# What's New

What each version of Realistic Physics changed, newest first. The game shows its version on the start screen and in its bottom-left corner, and every Short made from the game shows the version it was made with. Only the newest version is online.

## v0.15 (2026-10-19)

- A Fight page, opened by the Fight button on the start screen: you play the left fighter, punch, and hold to lean back so a punch falls short, while the right fighter spars on its own or a friend plays it.
- A fighter can lean back, away from the other: its upper body leans and its weight shifts away for as long as it is held, so a punch thrown at it falls short, and it comes back upright when let go.

## v0.14 (2026-10-19)

- You can choose one body alone instead of two fighters, to use as a punching bag: the 2 key, or the "2 fighters" button, switches between them.

## v0.13 (2026-10-19)

- Two fighters stand side by side. The left arrow key, or the LEFT punch button, makes the left fighter punch the right one, and the right arrow key, or RIGHT, the right fighter punch the left one, each with its arm nearer the other.
- A punch leans the body into it and reaches the other fighter's head over its guard, and lands as the bare fist does: it bruises and knocks the head aside, rarely splits the skin, and breaks no skull, forearm, or hand of the puncher's.
- Every tool strikes either fighter, and the fighters' bodies push against each other where they touch.
- A blow hurts the body the same wherever it stands and nearly the same on either side: a knife taken up on the body's right no longer turns round through the arm in its first slash.

## v0.12 (2026-10-18)

- The figure throws punches: the left and right arrow keys, or the LEFT and RIGHT punch buttons, make it punch with that arm, out to its side as fast as a hard real punch, and bring the arm back into its guard.
- A fourth tool, the bare fist (F), hits like a real punch: it bruises and knocks the head aside, rarely splits the skin, and breaks no skull or forearm.

## v0.11 (2026-10-18)

- A broken arm drops out of the guard and hangs at the side instead of staying raised: the elbow's flesh rests as a relaxed elbow does, and the inside of the elbow is a crease, so the arm can also straighten all the way without tearing.
- The muscles across the elbow hold a smashed arm together, so its broken bone stays inside it, and tear when it is pulled too far.
- Unbroken skin holds the pieces of a closed break in, so they poke out through it less often.

## v0.10.2 (2026-10-17)

- A blow hurts the body the same on every screen. A big window used to build a finer body with relatively smaller tools, and a small one a coarser body; now every screen plays the same body and only draws it larger or smaller.
- On a big screen the tools are drawn as big next to the body as on any other screen.
- Resizing the window fits the body to it again, even after the body has been hurt.

## v0.10.1 (2026-10-16)

- A hard blow to the shoulder no longer leaves the forearm's bone poking out of its skin: the arm's muscles hold their bones as firmly as braced muscle does.
- A dislocated joint keeps its bones close together, so a smashed arm's bones stay inside it.
- On a big screen the body no longer drops its arms out of the guard as the game starts.
- A flap of cut skin that falls against the body flies off less often.

## v0.10 (2026-10-14)

- Gravity is as strong as on Earth: the body, a limb that lets go, and blood fall as fast as they really would. Before, everything fell about three times too slowly.
- The body stands exactly as it is drawn: its joints hold the bones together like hinges, so the fists stay up in the guard instead of sagging a little under their weight.

## v0.9 (2026-10-14)

- The body stands in a fighting guard, fists up beside its head, and its flesh moves with its bones.
- Blows move it: a bat to the head snaps it aside and rocks the body, and a fist knocked out of the guard comes back.
- An arm or leg with a broken bone drops out of the guard and hangs.
- A broken bone stays under unbroken skin more often, and a hard bat no longer tears open the skin over a closed break.

## v0.8.2 (2026-10-13)

- How to install the game as an app now shows right under the Start button, so you see it first.

## v0.8.1 (2026-10-13)

- Firefox on Windows can now install the game as an app too, like Chrome, Edge and Safari, and the start screen shows how.

## v0.8 (2026-10-13)

- The game can be installed as an app with its own icon, and then opens from the home screen or the computer's apps without the browser's bars.
- The start screen shows how to install it in your browser, with an Install button where the browser allows one.

## v0.7 (2026-10-12)

- Skin, fat and muscle weigh and stretch as the real tissues do.
- Bone stays inside the hands and feet, and broken pieces of bone no longer fly out of a wound.
- The game shows its version on the start screen and in its bottom-left corner, and so do the Shorts made from it.

## v0.6 (2026-10-11)

- Injuries look as they really do, not exaggerated: blood comes only from wounds and is drawn at its real size, and a bruise reddens at once and darkens over a quarter of an hour.
- Crushed flesh shows as pulp instead of a hole through the body.
- Knife cuts open exactly where the blade went, and blows reach bone through the flesh over it.
- An about card tells what the simulation is, with links to how it was made.

## v0.5 (2026-10-09)

- Injuries follow published injury research: a firm sledgehammer swing can break the arm, and wounds bleed at real rates.
- Sound, made from what the simulation does.
- Knife cuts open into gaping wounds, fast blows split skin and muscle, and bones break where they bend most.
- Blood runs down the skin, drips off the body and pools on the floor.

## v0.4 (2026-10-06)

- A neutral mannequin body.
- Damage follows the speed of a blow: slow hits only bruise, while hard ones break bones and open wounds.
- Tools stay steady in the hand and move freely through wounds.
- The game starts with the sledgehammer in hand.

## v0.3 (2026-10-05)

- Plays in the browser.
- The sledgehammer, bat and knife have real shapes, and the knife cuts.
- The body takes the shape of a real human figure.

## v0.2 (2026-06-29)

- Rewritten in Rust, with a more human body shape, a rib cage, joints that can dislocate, bruised flesh, and cuts that spread along the skin.

## v0.1 (2026-06-20)

- The first version, written in C++: a body of skin, muscle and bone that blunt, sharp and heavy tools tear and break, with blood from the wounds.
